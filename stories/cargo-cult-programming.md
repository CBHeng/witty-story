# `cargo cult programming` — 貨物崇拜式編程 / cargo cult programming

## 它做什麼 / What it does

照著形式抄，不知道為什麼。沒人敢刪的設定、複製過來就跟著走的 try/catch、每次都照做卻說不出理由的那個步驟。它們看起來都對，而且從來不出錯 —— 因為它們根本沒有在做事。

Copying the shape of code without knowing why. The config nobody dares delete, the
try/catch that came along for the ride, the step you always perform and can't explain.
It all looks right and it never breaks, because none of it is doing anything.

## 那場演講 / The speech

1974 年，Feynman 在 Caltech 的[畢業典禮上講偽科學](https://calteches.library.caltech.edu/51/2/CargoCult.pdf)。他說南太平洋有一群人，戰爭期間看過飛機載著好東西降落，於是把跑道照樣排出來、兩側點上火、蓋一間小屋讓人坐在裡面，頭上戴兩塊木頭當耳機、插幾根竹子當天線 —— 他是塔台 —— 然後等飛機降落。

「他們每一件事都做對了。形式完美。看起來和當年一模一樣。但它不管用。沒有飛機降落。」

Caltech, 1974. Feynman is giving the [commencement
address](https://calteches.library.caltech.edu/51/2/CargoCult.pdf), and it is about
pseudoscience. In the South Seas, he says, there are people who saw airplanes land during
the war with lots of good materials, and who have since arranged to imitate things like
runways, to put fires along the sides of the runways, to make a wooden hut for a man to sit
in, with two wooden pieces on his head like headphones and bars of bamboo sticking out like
antennas — he's the controller — and they wait for the airplanes to land.

"They're doing everything right. The form is perfect. It looks exactly the way it looked
before. But it doesn't work. No airplanes land."

## 他真正在講的 / What he was actually talking about

貨物崇拜只是開場。他真正的例子是一條走廊和一群老鼠。

老鼠跑走廊，總能找到上次放過食物的那道門。有個叫 Young 的人想知道牠們靠什麼。他把整條走廊重新上漆，讓每道門看起來一模一樣 —— 老鼠還是找得到；他用化學藥劑蓋掉氣味 —— 還是找得到；他把走廊蓋起來 —— 還是找得到。最後他發現，老鼠靠的是踩上去時地板發出的聲音；要擋掉這個，只能把整條走廊架在沙上。

Young 做的不是老鼠實驗。他做的是那個「告訴你老鼠實驗必須控制哪些條件」的實驗。而後來的人繼續照老方法跑迷宮，沒有人把走廊架到沙上，也沒有人引用他的論文 —— 因為他沒有發現任何關於老鼠的事。

跑道在，火堆在，塔台裡的人也在。飛機不會降落。

The cargo cult was only the opening. His real example was a corridor full of rats.

Rats running a corridor could always find the door that had food behind it last time, and a
man named Young set out to learn how. He repainted the whole corridor so every door looked
identical; they still found it. He treated it with chemicals to kill the scent; they still
found it. He covered the corridor over; they still found it. In the end he discovered they
were going by the sound the floor made underfoot, and that the only way to kill that was to
set the whole corridor in sand.

Young had not run a rat experiment. He had run the experiment that tells you which
conditions a rat experiment has to control. And everyone after him went on running mazes the
old way. Nobody set a corridor in sand. Nobody cited his papers — because he had not
discovered anything about rats.

The runway is there. The fires are lit. The controller is in his hut. No airplanes land.

## 這個詞怎麼過來的 / How it crossed over

1991 年，Jargon File 收進 cargo cult programming，詞源直接寫 Feynman。2000 年 Steve McConnell 把它放大成整個產業的病：看到成功的公司導了某套流程就跟著導，看到那裡的人加班就規定大家加班 —— 把結果當成原因來模仿。

In 1991 the Jargon File took in cargo cult programming, crediting Feynman outright.
In 2000 Steve McConnell widened it into a whole industry's disease: a company adopts the
process a successful shop uses, or notices that people there work long hours and makes long
hours the rule — mistaking the results for the causes.

## 那個故事本身 / The story itself

而那個故事，沒有人回頭查過。

有完整紀錄的第一個貨物崇拜是 1919 年巴布亞灣的 Vailala Madness，比那場戰爭早了二十幾年，而他們等的是一艘載著祖靈回來的幽靈蒸汽船。最有名的 John Frum 運動，不晚於 1930 年代就在 Tanna 島上了，比美軍抵達還早 —— 而它的內容不是模仿白人，是把白人丟掉。島上實行的「Tanna Law」禁掉了舞蹈、kava 和所有傳統儀式；John Frum 要他們把錢丟進海裡、把孩子帶離教會學校、回去喝 kava、跳自己的舞。1941 年，領袖被押上船去坐牢。

「cargo cult」這個詞第一次印出來是 1945 年，一篇標題叫〈新幾內亞原住民戰後有暴動之虞嗎？〉的文章。它誕生的時候，是一則殖民地的治安警告。

至於木頭耳機和竹子天線 —— Tanna 島確實有過象徵性的跑道，這點是真的。但那組固定畫面最可能的[流通源頭](https://www.righto.com/2025/01/its-time-to-abandon-cargo-cult-metaphor.html)，是 1962 年一部叫《Mondo Cane》的獵奇紀錄片，片尾拍了 Port Moresby 機場上方山頭的竹飛機和竹塔台。

Feynman 整篇文章要求的是：去做那個沒有人做過的複製實驗，把所有可能讓你錯的東西攤開來。而他的開場故事，從來沒有人對它做過這件事。一代一代轉述下去，寫進詞彙表，寫進期刊 —— 形式完美，飛機沒有降落。

他的第一原則，第一個抓到的是這個故事本身。

Nobody went back and checked the story.

The first well-documented cargo cult was the Vailala Madness, in the Papuan Gulf in 1919 —
more than twenty years before the war Feynman meant — and what it waited for was a ghost
steamer crewed by the returning dead. The famous one, John Frum on Tanna, was there no later
than the 1930s, before the Americans were. And it was not an imitation of white men; it was
a refusal of them. Under the Tanna Law the Presbyterian mission had banned dancing, kava and
every custom the island had. John Frum told people to throw their money into the sea, take
their children out of the mission schools, drink kava again and dance their own dances. In
1941 the leaders were shipped off to jail.

"Cargo cult" reached print in 1945, in an article titled "Is There Danger of a Post-war
Flare-up Among New Guinea Natives?" It began life as a colonial warning about unrest.

As for the wooden headphones and the bamboo antennas — Tanna really did have symbolic
landing strips; that much is on record. But the fixed image most likely [reached
us](https://www.righto.com/2025/01/its-time-to-abandon-cargo-cult-metaphor.html) through
*Mondo Cane*, a 1962 shockumentary that closes on a bamboo airplane and a bamboo control
tower on a mountainside above the Port Moresby airfield.

What Feynman's essay asks for is that you run the replication nobody has run, and lay out
everything that could show you wrong. His opening story is the one nobody ever did that to.
It got passed along, written into a glossary, written into a journal — the form perfect, and
no airplanes landing.

The first thing his first principle catches is the story itself.

## 我也在裡面 / I was in it too

有一段時間我追的是技術本身：哪個紅就學哪個，形式抄得很齊，很少回頭問它當初是為了解決什麼，才長成那個樣子。

後來換我坐在面試的另一邊，問「為什麼選 MongoDB」，答案是「因為它很知名」。不只面試 —— 平常跟同事聊技術、討論選型的時候，同一種回答也一直出現。我很熟悉這個答案，因為這是我自己提過、也被提過的答案。

最難的是第三件。知道這個詞以後，它很容易變成我拿去量別人的一把尺 —— 而量的時候，我忘了自己也曾經無知，也忘了他們只是還沒茁壯。那正是同一個病的下一種型態：把「我知道什麼是 cargo cult」本身當成正確的形式。

這個詞從來不是拿來量別人的。它是拿來督促自己的：隨時保持專注，持續練習，不盲從。

前兩件，可以從優秀的 mentor 帶領與那份自律救回來的。但第三件沒有人救得了，只能一直記得保持謙虛。

For a stretch I was chasing the technology itself: whatever was hot, learn it, use it,
rarely going back to ask what problem had made it that shape in the first place. The form
was complete. The reason was empty.

Later I was the one on the other side of the interview. "Why MongoDB?" — "Because it's well
known." And not only in interviews: the same answer kept surfacing whenever colleagues and I
talked through a choice of tools. I know that answer well. I have given it, and I have been
given it.

The third one is the hardest. Once you know the term it turns very easily into a ruler I
hold up against other people — and while I am holding it, I forget that I once knew nothing
either, and that they are simply not there yet. Which is the same disease in its next form:
treating "I know what a cargo cult is" as the correct shape to have.

The term was never for measuring anyone else. It is there to keep me working: stay
attentive, keep practicing, don't follow a form just because it is the form.

There is a way out of the first two: the guidance of a good mentor, and that discipline. But
there is no way out of the third. You just have to keep remembering to be humble.

## 致敬 / Credit

Richard Feynman（1918–1988）。「第一原則是不要騙自己 —— 而你是最好騙的那一個」這句話是他說的，而它對他自己講的故事一樣有效。

Richard Feynman (1918–1988). "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself — and
you are the easiest person to fool" is his line, and it holds against his own stories too.
